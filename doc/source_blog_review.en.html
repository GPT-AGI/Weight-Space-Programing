Weight-space programming · Experiment 8 reviewed

Astra's Qwen3.5-0.8B math edit:
blog claims vs. public logs

Based on the authors' complete public experiment logs. Every English quotation is reproduced verbatim from Astra's own replies; descriptions in the blog, README, and user messages are not attributed to Astra.

7.9→48.1%① MATH-500 accuracy in the best run (four samples per problem)
50 calls GPU forwards used by Astra to generate answers and read internal activations; the issue was observed from model runs
131,072① parameters changed: 64 MLP columns in each of layers 15 and 19
7→65%① MATH-500 completion rate; accuracy among completed answers fell from 93% to 71%

TL;DR

  1. Astra worked in a well-tooled interpretability lab; it did not infer the answer by inspecting weights alone. The harness let Astra run the model, record internal activations, and perform A/B comparisons, with 50 experimental calls. Astra arrived at the result through successive experiments, and its first hypothesis did not produce a significant result.
  2. The issue was found by running the model and examining activations; the repair was a standard mathematical projection. Astra compared the model's internal states during productive reasoning and stalled loops, identified a “loop direction,” and used a formula to subtract that direction from two layers.
  3. The “6×” gain came primarily from finishing answers rather than improved mathematical ability. The original model used the full token budget on 93% of problems. After the edit, completion rose from 7% to 65%, while accuracy among completed answers fell from 93% to 71%①.

Checking the blog's claims

The left column quotes the blog verbatim (retrieved 2026-11-03); the right checks each claim against the public logs. This page covers Experiment 8 (math) only. The blog's claim about identifying a network's training objective from weights alone concerns Experiment 1 and is outside this review's scope.

Claim Blog textWhat the logs show
“Astra identifies an issue in its weights causing reasoning loops”Qwen3.5-0.8B's main failure on these math problems was a reasoning loop: it repeated passages during thinking until it exhausted the token limit without answering. Astra found this by using GPU forward tools to run the model and compare internal activations on productive and looping text. The issue was observed in model behavior and activations, not inferred from weights alone.
“fixes this by natively editing 0.016% of the weights”In Python, Astra applied W ← W − α·u·uᵀW to subtract a “loop direction” from MLP outputs in layers 15 and 19. It changed 64 columns per layer, for 2 × 64 × 1,024 = 131,072 parameters①. The percentage depends on which parameter total is used as the denominator; it mainly describes the edit's shape—two layers and 64 columns per layer—not its precision. The blog itself writes: much like removing a behavior by deleting a single direction from a model’s weights, placing it in the same family as the 2024 refusal-direction ablation.
“this weight correction raises MATH-500 from 7.9% to 48.1%”The numbers check out: 7.9% → 48.1%①. Completion rose from 7% to 65%, while accuracy among completed answers fell from 93% to 71%①. The gain came from no longer getting stuck.
“generalizes out of the box to instruction following, raising IFEval by 3x”The numbers check out: 21.0 → 62.3①. The blog itself writes: The loops are not special to math, so performance beyond math improved as well. This is consistent with repairing a general looping defect, rather than learning a new instruction-following capability.
“asked to make Qwen3.5-0.8B better at math without gradients”Accurate. Mean-difference directions and directional ablation do not require gradients. Astra used 50 GPU forwards—generating answers and reading internal activations—to conduct the experiments.
“well past what we’d expect from humans or conventional algorithms”Every step used in Experiment 8 is a standard method developed by human researchers in 2022–2024: mean-difference directions, direct attribution, directional ablation, dose-response tests, random controls, and held-out validation after freezing the edit.

A more precise description of “natively reading and writing weights” is: fluently running a standard interpretability pipeline in a well-tooled experimental environment, then using code to write a projection formula into the weights. The reading relies on activations and experiments; the writing relies on formulas and code.

What the authors' harness gave Astra

Authors' notes Sources: the Experiment 8 README, the round-one prompt.txt for p50c, and the harness directory in the logs repository. Every number below was checked against the source.

  • A persistent Python environment (CPU) It could read and write any model weight, save changes with save_edit, and execute code up to 300 times.
  • GPU forward tools with budgets of 10, 25, or 50 calls A single generation call could produce up to 512 answers. In “both” mode, original and edited models each answered the same 256 problems with matched seeds, and the tool returned the accuracy difference, standard error, and truncation rate directly.
  • Up to 1,024 text segments per read Each segment could be up to 32,768 tokens, with per-token log probabilities, hidden states, MLP activations, and attention returned.
  • 15,458 practice problems with answers 7,469 GSM8K, 7,275 MATH, and 714 AIME problems.
  • Notes, handoffs, and guardrails Astra had to keep notes.md current. Every 40 rounds or after a restart, a new session received the task, Astra's notes, and the current edit. save_edit rejected edits to the final normalization layer, embedding/output layers, and whole-tensor scalar multiplication.

This is a complete mechanistic-interpretability workbench: it records internal activations, supports same-problem and same-seed A/B evaluation, and provides a persistent analysis environment.

## Final choice
    
    Retained the frozen edits in `/work/edited`, changing only **64 MLP output columns each in layers 15 and 19**—131,072 weight values total. The edits counteract measured repetition directions; embeddings, output head, and final norm remain untouched.
    
    ## Diagnosis and evidence
    
    The dominant failure was repetitive thinking that exhausted the token budget. Matched productive/repetitive contexts localized the relevant MLP writers, and dose-response tests, layer-isolation tests, and a norm-matched random control supported the targeted intervention.
    
    **Independent paired confirmation: 1,152 held-out practice problems**
    
    | Source | Original | Edited |
    |---|---:|---:|
    | GSM8K practice | 11.6% | **70.8%** |
    | MATH practice | 9.5% | **50.0%** |
    | Combined | 10.6% | **60.4%** |
    
    The combined gain was **49.8 percentage points, SE 1.5**, with **579 rescues and five regressions**. Of those rescues, 576 were originally truncated outputs, while aggregate accuracy on originally finished outputs was unchanged. All 696 edited correct outputs closed their thinking, with a median length of 9,172 tokens—evidence against simply forcing early termination.
    
    These are practice results, not unseen benchmark scores; mathematical errors, missing boxes, and some degeneration remain.
    
    **Evidence:** `/work/final_report.md`  
    **Exact neuron indices and hashes:** `/work/frozen_weights_manifest.json`
+49.8 on 1,152 practice problems is Astra's self-reported figure; the independent evaluation appears next.
